Project pipeline

  1. 01 Assemble the panel

    Join hourly rides and NOAA weather in UTC. Use CAMS hourly AQI for 80.3% of hours, with EPA daily AQI as fallback. The panel has 26,288 hours.

  2. 02 Define the target

    Estimate expected rides from 2022–2023 season, weekday/weekend and hour groups, adjusted for training-year volume. Keep hours with at least 20 expected rides; low demand means actual rides below 50% of expected.

    This gives $active_hours active hours with 12 model features.

  3. 03 Refit and score in the browser

    Refit unweighted logistic regression on all $active_hours active hours and export the scaler and 12-feature coefficients. The browser derives features and applies these fixed parameters to manual conditions or forecasts.

    Manual input produces one estimate. Forecasts compare hours in the next 24 hours, 06:00–21:00 DC time, and select the lowest eligible probability.

    Forecast comparisons exclude hours with missing inputs, heat index ≥103°F or AQI ≥150.

  4. 04 Check the research results

    Fit five models on $train_hours hours in 2022–2023; evaluate on $test_hours hours in 2024. Report curves, scores and whole-day bootstrap intervals for the unweighted model, with sensitivity analyses.

    Simulate same-day shifts of up to three hours using historical unweighted predictions. Study AQI and smoke associations in the 2022–2024 DC records. Refit separately on Seoul data with 10 features and a 75/25 whole-date split within each season.

These research checks use separate fits and analyses. The historical unweighted model overestimates probabilities; live forecast accuracy and actual ride recovery remain unmeasured. See the full pipeline and code references.
