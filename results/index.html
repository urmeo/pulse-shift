From public data to browser estimates

  1. 01 Assemble the panel

    Join hourly rides and NOAA weather in UTC. Use CAMS hourly AQI for 80.3% of hours, with EPA daily AQI as fallback. The panel has 26,288 hours.

  2. 02 Define the target

    Estimate expected rides from 2022–2023 season, weekday/weekend and hour groups, adjusted for training-year volume. Keep hours with at least 20 expected rides; low demand means actual rides below 50% of expected.

    This gives 24,354 active hours with 12 model features.

  3. 03 Evaluate the models

    Fit five models on 16,150 hours in 2022–2023; evaluate on 8,204 hours in 2024. Report curves, scores and whole-day bootstrap intervals for the unweighted model, with sensitivity analyses.

  4. 04 Refit for the browser

    Refit unweighted logistic regression on all 24,354 active hours and export its 12-feature coefficients. Enter conditions manually or compare forecasts for the next 24 hours, 06:00–21:00 DC time.

    Forecast comparisons exclude hours with missing inputs, heat index ≥103°F or AQI ≥150.

AQI associations use the 2022–2024 records. Seoul uses a separate city-specific refit. See the full pipeline and code references.

Time-shift scenario

A shift of up to three hours produced a predicted recovery of 36.8% of modeled lost rides (95% interval 33.0%–40.9%). This assumes full transfer to the chosen hours. At an assumed 50% transfer efficiency, recovery is 18.4%.
